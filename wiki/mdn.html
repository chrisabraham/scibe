MDN

MDN is the name this edition’s editor gives the company behind The Meta Network. His ruling, given while this context layer was being built, is plain: “MDN was the company behind TMN.” He speaks from inside the story: in 1994 he worked in the same Arlington building as the network’s operators, and the name he gives is the name as he knew it in those rooms. This entry prints MDN on that basis, and prints the documentary record beside it, because the two do not quite match, and this wiki does not smooth over a mismatch. The entry is built, though, on the one place where the work itself touches the company--and that place is a lexia.

Lexia 49 stands under the editor’s own header--“20:44) Chris Abraham 20-OCT-94”, the header as it stands at the head of the lexia in this edition--and in it he is at his desk, wired on coffee, writing from work. The text prints the address outright: “here at 2000 15th Street North, Suite 107, Arlington, VA 22201”. That is this work’s own stake in the company question, and it is textual, not remembered: a co-author’s office address, printed in the work in 1994, in the same building where the documented record places the company itself--Metasystems Design Group at Suite 103, four doors down. The address is in the work because a writer of the work worked next door to the network’s operators; everything else this entry says is citation around that printed fact.

His employer in Suite 107 was Picture Network International (PNI): the job was his first after college--George Washington University, May 1993--and his published professional profile dates it June 1993 to October 1994, in technical customer support for PNI’s Seymour platform, the online stock-photography service that put keyword search and thumbnail results in front of picture buyers. By the editor’s account, that is the whole of the connection: PNI had nothing to do with TMN, The WELL, the ARTIST conference, or MDN--only adjacent office space in the same building--and what he knew of that world he knew socially, from the smokers at the front-entrance patio; but for the smoking habit, he allows, he might never have known it at all.

The documented record, in brief, names the company Metasystems Design Group: MDG’s own press release of March 28, 1983 announced TMN’s opening under that name, and the era’s directories and trade press follow it. The working name in the rooms was the other styling. Virginia “Ginny” Little, the Kalamazoo teacher who taught through TMN’s conferencing, published her program account in 1997 naming the company “Metasystems Design Network (Metanet)”, and the editor’s November 1999 scrape of TMN’s classroom conferences runs on “Metanet” more than 110 times, in many voices--when the company changed hands it is still the name Little reaches for: “I am negotiating with metanet now.” The full documentary parade--the directory listings, InfoWorld, the Screen Porch backgrounder, the Wilson Boulevard chapter--and the Little passage and the scrape evidence in full stand in Item 8’s MDN entry, on that work’s home ground; they are cited here, not re-argued. No consulted source expands, or uses, the initials MDN. Shown Little’s text and the scrape’s usage, the editor affirms the identification outright: “Yes. Metanet. The Meta Network is tmn.com.”

For Scibe, though, the company is not only a name to rule on; it is the work’s fate in one of its two copies. The ARTS WIRE edition of the work was served from the company’s domain--tmn.com carried ARTS WIRE’s pages in the web years--and when that world ended, those addresses died with it: the ARTS WIRE copy survives only as the dead pointers lexia 66 prints. The WELL copy, kept on Malloy’s pages there, survives entire. The pieces of that story stand in this site’s MDG and TMN entries and in the colophon; the MDN entry is where a reader looks for the company, so it is stated here once, plainly: one of this work’s two first homes was this company’s server, and that is why only one of the two is still standing.

What stays open is narrower than the whole question, and it stays open. Which styling the company itself used, and when, the surviving paper does not settle; and whether MDN was ever the company’s own printed short form, rather than the form one building’s memory kept, no consulted source says. This entry does not invent the connection between the initials and either styling. The question stands open, printed as such, for the editor’s final settlement; the documented company has its own entry under its documented name. Until the paper settles it, this entry keeps all three names in view, each at its own weight: MDN in its title, on the editor’s ruling; Metasystems Design Group in the record; and Metanet between them, in the rooms’ usage.
